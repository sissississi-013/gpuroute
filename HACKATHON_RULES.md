## Tracks (pick ONE)

### Track 1: Hacking with Public Data APIs
Build something interesting with publicly available APIs or datasets: an MCP server, a CLI, a web app, or something unexpected. Show creative ways to turn public data into something useful.

### Track 2: Personal Agents & Data Privacy
Explore where personal AI agents, data privacy and user consent meet. Build agents that empower individuals while giving them meaningful control over their data.

## Rules
1. **Choose one track.** Each team competes in one of the two tracks.
2. **Build during the hackathon.** Development happens during the event. Existing libraries, frameworks, APIs and dev tools are allowed.
3. **Use any tech stack.** No required language, framework or AI model.
4. **Team participation.** Individuals or teams.
5. **Original work.** Must be your team's own work. Open-source tools and AI-assisted development are permitted; **credit external contributions**.
6. **Respect data privacy.** Use publicly accessible or properly authorized data. Don't expose private information or use data without permission.
7. **Working demo required.** Demo a functional prototype live during judging.
8. **Submission deadline.** Submit on Devpost by 5:00 PM on Oct 22. Late submissions may not be judged.
9. **Judging.** Projects are judged separately within each track. Judges' decisions are final.
10. **Code of conduct.** Be respectful, collaborate responsibly, follow venue guidelines.

## Submission requirements (Devpost)
- **Project name & description:** what you built, the problem it solves, why it matters
- **Track:** Public Data APIs *or* Personal Agents & Data Privacy
- **Source code:** GitHub (or other accessible) repo link
- **Presentation:** short slide deck covering the idea, solution and technical implementation
- **Demo:** working prototype shown live

## Live presentation
**5 minutes total** per team: pitch + live demo + judge Q&A.

## Judging criteria
1. **User Value & Impact:** Does it solve a clear problem for a specific user? Would someone find it useful, and why?
2. **Technical Execution:** Does the core workflow work? Are the implementation and integrations appropriate for the problem?
3. **Originality & Creativity:** A fresh idea, an inventive combination of tools or data, or a meaningful improvement on an existing approach?
4. **Track Alignment & Responsible Data Use:** How well does it address its track and handle data, privacy and consent?
5. **Usability & Demo Clarity:** Is it understandable and usable for its audience? Does the demo clearly show how it works and what it achieves?
